# Research: Filipino MSMEs

Oct 22, 2026 · Prepared for @Ethan and team, CSSprint 2026 · Built in \~10 min, verify anything you'll cite on stage

## 1. The brief, decoded

**The literal ask:** build a practical, digital solution to ONE specific problem that Filipino MSMEs face. Not "help MSMEs" in general.

**Required elements**

- Digital: software someone actually uses, not a campaign or a poster
- Practical: works for a real Filipino business owner today, with the phone and budget they have
- Specific problem: one named pain, not an all-in-one platform
- Filipino MSMEs: the user or beneficiary is a micro, small or medium enterprise
- Event deliverables still apply: GitHub repo, deck, 1-2 min video, live demo for Top 10

**Words that could be read different ways**

- **"MSME":** (a) micro, meaning sari-sari stores, carinderias, market stalls, home sellers (90% of all establishments) vs (b) small and medium, meaning registered firms with staff, BIR obligations and some budget. Very different users.
- **"Specific problem":** (a) one narrow pain for one type of business (utang tracking at a sari-sari store) vs (b) one problem category shared across many MSMEs (access to credit).
- **"Faced by":** (a) the MSME owner is the user of the app vs (b) the MSME benefits, but the user is someone serving them (a coop loan officer, Negosyo Center staff, an insurer).
- **"Practical":** (a) easy for a low-tech owner to adopt with zero training vs (b) realistic to run as a business (someone pays, it can scale).

**Possible traps**

- A generic "MSME super app" with ten features. Fails "specific".
- A directory of DTI programs or business tips. Out of scope as a static info site.
- "Help MSMEs go viral" without a real tool. Out of scope as a marketing campaign.
- A generic business-advice chatbot. Out of scope as AI without a clear need.
- A clone of Packworks or GrowSari with no new angle. Judges from banking may know them.
- Anything that needs a live bank, BIR or Facebook integration you can't get approved in 2 days.

**Questions to ask organizers**

- Can the main user be an institution that serves MSMEs (lender, LGU, Negosyo Center), or must it be the MSME itself?
- Is the scoring rubric published, and how much weight goes to the business model vs the tech?
- Do they want proof of talking to a real MSME owner?

## 2. Problem landscape

MSMEs are almost every business in the country, most are tiny, and the formal system (banks, insurers, BIR) runs on records they don't keep.

**Key facts**

- **Scale:** 1,241,733 MSMEs in 2023, which is 99.63% of all enterprises. Micro alone is 90.43% (1,127,058). ([DTI, from PSA 2023 List of Establishments](https://dti.gov.ph/businesses/msmes/msmeresources/msme-statistics))
- **Jobs:** MSMEs created 6,351,466 jobs, 66.97% of total employment, in 2023. (same source)
- **Where they are:** top MSME sectors in 2023 were wholesale/retail trade (604,313), accommodation and food (190,899) and manufacturing (140,180). (same source)
- **Credit gap:** bank loans to MSMEs passed P580B but are only about 6% of all business lending. (BSP via [Context.ph, Sept 2026](https://context.ph/2026/09/12/bsp-flags-msme-credit-gap-as-loans-reach-p580b/), news report)
- **Who borrows from banks:** in a BCG survey of 3,098 MSMEs (Nov 2024), fewer than 10% said they have access to bank loans. Fear of debt and high interest were the main reasons. ([Malaya, 2025](https://malaya.com.ph/business/business-news/msmes-turn-more-optimistic-but-fear-access-to-credit))
- **The cost of the alternative:** "5-6" lenders charge about 20% per 60 days, roughly 120% a year, per a Quezon City carinderia owner's account. ([Inquirer](https://business.inquirer.net/?p=204763), older news report)
- **No records:** in a 2026 study of 18 rural micro-businesses in Bohol, 14 (77.78%) had no formal record-keeping and relied on memory. ([RSIS, May 2026](https://rsisinternational.org/journals/ijriss/article.php?id=9265), small single study)
- **Digital payments are here:** 64.7% of retail payment volume was digital in 2025, and QR Ph processed 2.47B transactions worth P1.16T. But only 18.75% of business payments were digital. (BSP 2025 reports via [Daily Tribune](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/08/19/bsp-digital-payments-account-for-nearly-two-thirds-of-retail-volume) and [BusinessWorld summary](https://pro.edgex.exchange/en-US/news/article/philippines-qr-ph-digital-payments-growth-2025))
- **Want vs do:** 77% of MSMEs said they intend to use digital tools more, but only 20% actually increased usage. (BCG survey, Malaya 2025)

**Local context**

- Sari-sari stores extend credit (utang) as a way to keep customers. Many owners put up "No Credit" signs and still lend.
- Owners often mix store cash and household cash. One Leyte owner in a Hapinoy program said she didn't know her real earnings for this reason. ([Inquirer](https://business.inquirer.net/181741/mothers-learn-small-changes-make-a-big-impact), older)
- Over 70% of MSMEs know DTI programs like Negosyo Centers and Kapatid Mentor ME, but actual participation is often below 20%. (BCG via [Inquirer Plus](https://plus.inquirer.net/?p=329830))
- The Philippines sits in the typhoon belt, and most MSMEs are uninsured against disaster-related disruption. ([MicroSave Consulting, 2025](https://www.microsave.net/signature_projects/client-centric-insurance-for-msmes-in-the-philippines/))

**Why now**

- **BSP credit push (Sept 2026):** BSP is pushing a Standard Business Loan Application Form and a Credit Risk Database with JICA that uses business data and scoring for borrowers with thin files. (Context.ph)
- **Carinderia squeeze (Oct 2026):** the carinderia owners' association says rising LPG and ingredient costs are forcing price hikes, smaller servings or fewer dishes. ([Daily Tribune, Oct 5 2026](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/10/05/lpg-price-surge-squeezes-small-eateries))
- **BIR e-invoicing deadline Dec 31, 2026:** e-commerce businesses classed small, medium or large must issue structured e-invoices. Micro taxpayers are exempt. ([KPMG on RR 26-2025](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2025/10/philippines-e-invoicing-compliance-deadline-extended.html))
- **Online seller withholding:** platforms withhold 1% on half of remittances for sellers above P500,000 a year. ([Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1961672/bir-online-sellers-must-now-pay-withholding-tax))
- **Government AI push:** DTI staff were trained in March 2026 to help MSMEs adopt simple AI tools, under the UN Digital PINAS program. ([ILO](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/trade-officials-complete-training-introduce-ai-tools-enterprises))

**Gaps that keep showing up:**

- Most tools assume the owner will do daily data entry. Few owners keep it up.
- The formal system (banks, insurers, BIR) needs records, and micro owners have none in a usable form.
- Many funded players serve the supply side (brands, distributors) more than the owner's own decisions.

## 4. People and pains

Six groups, from the owner at the counter to the people who are supposed to help them.

**1. Sari-sari store owner (often a mother or a senior)**

- **The pain:** doesn't know if the store actually earns. Utang slips away. Store cash gets used for household needs.
- **Evidence:** one study names lack of record keeping as the main thing that hurts sari-sari survival ([Academia](https://www.academia.edu/39643663/)). Bohol study: 77.78% keep no formal records.
- **Current workaround:** a notebook listahan, memory, a "No Credit" sign.

**2. Carinderia or small eatery owner**

- **The pain:** LPG, pork and rice keep going up. Has to decide between raising prices, shrinking servings or dropping dishes, usually by gut feel.
- **Evidence:** the carinderia owners' association flagged the LPG squeeze in Oct 2026. PSA reported restaurant inflation of 4.1% in Jan 2026, driven by meat viands like adobo and sinigang ([GMA](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/money/economy/975528/philippine-restaurants-inflation-january/story/)).
- **Current workaround:** raise prices by feel, switch to firewood for some cooking, borrow when cash runs short.

**3. Micro owner who needs working capital**

- **The pain:** a bank wants collateral and a credit history. 5-6 wants nothing but costs \~120% a year.
- **Evidence:** BCG says fewer than 10% have bank loan access. BSP says MSME loans are \~6% of business lending. A Quezon City carinderia owner described repaying P60,000 on a P50,000 loan in 60 days, collected daily (Inquirer).
- **Current workaround:** 5-6, family and friends, personal savings (44% rely on savings as main funding, per BCG).

**4. Online and Facebook Live seller**

- **The pain:** orders come in as "mine" comments and Messenger chats, confirmed one by one. As they grow, BIR registration and withholding rules kick in.
- **Evidence:** sellers still capture live orders manually from comments and confirm in Messenger ([BigSeller](https://www.bigseller.pro/blog/articleDetails/4531/mastering-facebook-live-orders-2026-tips-for-filipino-sellers.htm)). Withholding applies above P500,000 a year.
- **Current workaround:** screenshots, spreadsheets, or a paid order tool.

**5. The person on the other side of the desk (easy to overlook)**

- **Who:** a coop or microfinance loan officer, or Negosyo Center staff.
- **The pain:** has to judge or help a business that has no records, using interviews, site visits and paper forms.
- **Evidence:** BSP is building a credit risk database because lenders lack borrower data. DTI is training its own staff to help MSMEs use AI tools.
- **Current workaround:** site visits, character references, paper checklists.

**6. An MSME right after a typhoon (easy to overlook)**

- **The pain:** shop is closed, stock is damaged, no insurance, and no records to prove losses to a lender or aid program.
- **Evidence:** most MSMEs are uninsured (MicroSave 2025). After Yolanda, only about 30% of affected enterprises renewed their licenses ([BusinessWorld](https://www.bworldonline.com/?p=115375), older, 2018).
- **Current workaround:** savings, family, informal loans. One study found these only bridged an 8-10 day interruption ([MicroSave](https://www.microsave.net/?p=13556), older).

## 5. Opportunity territories

Six spaces, in no particular order. They differ by user, by stage of the problem, or by who holds the lever. None is ranked.

**Territory 1: How might we help micro store owners see their real profit and utang, without asking them to start bookkeeping?**

- **Why it matters:** no records is the root of the sari-sari survival problem (section 4, group 1). Owners mix household and store cash and can't tell what they earn.
- **The gap:** existing apps need daily manual entry. The notebook stays the real system.
- **Rough seeds:**
  - something that turns the owner's existing listahan into a running picture, without a new habit
  - a way to see when a suki's utang is drifting toward "never paid"
  - something that separates "kinuha para sa bahay" from actual store sales
- **Building blocks:** vision-capable LLMs for reading handwriting (test on Taglish notes first), PayMongo static QR Ph for logging cashless sales ([docs](https://docs.paymongo.com/docs/payment-acceptance-qr-ph)).
- **Open questions:** will an owner photograph or record anything daily? How messy is a real listahan page?
- **Lens:** strong user and pain. AI is only justified if it removes data entry (reading messy notes, voice). Risk: overlaps Packworks and SukliPH, so the angle must be clearly different. Data-minded judges (Nathaniel, Rix) will ask how accurate it is.

**Territory 2: How might we turn an informal business's everyday traces into something a lender can trust, so owners can leave 5-6?**

- **Why it matters:** fewer than 10% have bank access, MSME loans are \~6% of business lending, and 5-6 costs \~120% a year (section 2).
- **The gap:** BSP's form and credit database still assume records exist. The owner has GCash/QR history, a notebook and a reputation, none of it lender-ready.
- **Rough seeds:**
  - something that turns a few months of e-wallet receipts and notebook pages into a lender-ready picture
  - a way for a coop loan officer to see a store's cash rhythm without a site visit
  - a "how much can I safely borrow and pay back daily" view for the owner
- **Building blocks:** BSP Standard Business Loan Application Form fields (look up the form), PayMongo test-mode transaction data, realistic seed data.
- **Open questions:** would a coop or bank actually accept this? How do we handle consent and RA 10173? How do we explain the scoring logic plainly?
- **Lens:** plays straight to the Citi, City Savings Bank and BPI backgrounds on the panel, and to Abacus and Sun Life. Risk: a credit score that looks like a black box. If simple rules can score it, AI should do the messy reading, not the deciding.

**Territory 3: How might we help small eateries price and portion their menu as ingredient and LPG costs move every week?**

- **Why it matters:** carinderia margins are being squeezed right now (Oct 2026), and restaurant inflation was driven by meat viands (section 4, group 2).
- **The gap:** costing sheets exist for restaurants, but carinderia owners cook by feel and buy at the palengke without receipts.
- **Rough seeds:**
  - something that recomputes what one serving of adobo costs when pork goes up
  - a way to spot the ulam that is quietly losing money
  - a weekly "raise, shrink or swap" nudge in the owner's own words
- **Building blocks:** public price monitoring (check the DA and PSA price data, not verified yet), an LLM for turning loose recipe descriptions into ingredient lists.
- **Open questions:** do owners know their recipes in quantities? Would they trust a suggestion to raise prices?
- **Lens:** very timely and easy to demo with one persona. Risk: costing math is a spreadsheet. AI only earns its place if it handles the messy input (spoken recipes, palengke receipts).

**Territory 4: How might we help social and live sellers turn chaotic comment-and-chat orders into clean records as they grow?**

- **Why it matters:** live selling still runs on "mine" comments and one-by-one Messenger confirmation. Withholding and invoicing rules tighten as sellers grow (section 2, why now).
- **The gap:** paid tools (EasyStore, BigSeller) serve sellers already on marketplaces. The home seller doing ukay or kakanin on Facebook has nothing cheap.
- **Rough seeds:**
  - something that turns a pile of Taglish chats into confirmed orders and a packing list
  - a way for a seller to see they're nearing the P500,000 line before it surprises them
  - records that fall out of the conversation instead of being typed
- **Building blocks:** PayMongo checkout and payment links ([docs](https://developers.paymongo.com/docs/payment-acceptance-introduction)). Facebook's APIs need app review, so plan to mock the chat input.
- **Open questions:** is the gap real for micro sellers, or are they fine with screenshots? Note e-invoicing exempts micro taxpayers.
- **Lens:** AI fits naturally (parsing messy Taglish chat). Risk: competing with funded tools, and the demo depends on a mocked Facebook integration.

**Territory 5: How might we help MSMEs prepare for typhoons and recover faster after one?**

- **Why it matters:** most MSMEs are uninsured, savings bridge only \~8-10 days of closure, and many never reopen (section 4, group 6).
- **The gap:** insurance products exist but uptake is low. After a disaster, the owner has no record to show an insurer, lender or aid program.
- **Rough seeds:**
  - something that builds a loss record from photos the day after
  - a "what would 10 days closed cost me" view based on the owner's own numbers
  - a way to bundle cover with something the owner already uses daily
- **Building blocks:** PAGASA or public weather data (check what's open), a vision LLM for damage photos, insurer product info.
- **Open questions:** do micro owners want insurance at all? Who pays: the owner, an LGU, an insurer as a lead source?
- **Lens:** Sun Life (sponsor) and insurance angles fit. Risk: hard to show the pain live in a demo, since there's no typhoon on stage. Needs a strong story.

**Territory 6: How might we help the people who serve MSMEs (Negosyo Centers, coops, lenders) reach the owners who never show up?**

- **Why it matters:** over 70% know the programs, under 20% join. DTI is training its staff on AI tools in 2026 (section 2).
- **The gap:** programs need forms and documents. Owners don't have time or confidence to fill them.
- **Rough seeds:**
  - something that turns a 5-minute talk with an owner into a filled-in program or loan application
  - a way for staff to see which stores in a barangay qualify for which program
  - a follow-up loop so the owner doesn't drop out after the first visit
- **Building blocks:** DTI program eligibility rules (look them up), speech-to-text plus an LLM for form filling.
- **Open questions:** does the brief count an institution as the user? Would DTI or a coop adopt a student-built tool?
- **Lens:** the clearest "who pays" (an LGU, a coop or DTI). Risk: the MSME is the beneficiary, not the user, so ask organizers if that reading is OK. Also risks becoming an info site if the form-filling isn't real.

## 6. Tensions to decide

Only the team can call these. Each side buys you something different.

- **Micro vs small/medium:** micro (sari-sari, carinderia) is 90% of MSMEs, relatable and easy to demo. Small/medium has BIR and invoicing pains and can actually pay for software.
- **Owner as user vs institution as user:** the owner is the clearest reading of "faced by Filipino MSMEs". A lender, coop or Negosyo Center gives the clearest answer to "who pays?".
- **AI at the core vs plain logic:** reading messy notes, voice or chat is a real wow and plays to the AI judges. Simple math and rules are more reliable and dodge the "AI without a clear need" rule.
- **Fit into what owners already use vs a new app:** notebook, GCash, Messenger are already in their hands. A new app gives you full control of the demo but faces the 77% intend / 20% actually do gap.
- **Daily-habit tool vs one-time moment:** a daily tool (tracking, pricing) shows long-term value. A one-time moment (loan application, typhoon recovery) has a sharper story and a clearer wow.
- **Crowded space vs quiet niche:** sari-sari has funded players to compare against. Eateries and post-disaster recovery are emptier, but you have to prove the pain harder.

## 9. Territory 4 deep dive: live and social sellers

Live selling in the Philippines is huge and growing, it still runs on manual "mine" comments and chat, and its biggest money leaks happen **after** the live (no-pay claimers, fake payment proofs, COD returns). Most existing tools stop at capturing the claim.

**How big it is**

- PH digital economy on track for **US$36B GMV in 2025**, with e-commerce growing 16% a year. **Video commerce sellers hit 475,000, up 90% year-on-year**, with 1.2 billion video commerce transactions. Fashion, accessories, beauty and personal care are half of video commerce GMV. (e-Conomy SEA report by Google, Temasek and Bain, via [SunStar, Nov 2025](https://www.sunstar.com.ph/cebu/ph-digital-economy-surge-on-video-commerce-boom))
- Facebook reached **95.8M users** in the Philippines in late 2025, and TikTok reached 64.0M adults. These are ad-audience estimates, not active users. ([DataReportal Digital 2026](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-philippines))
- **47% of Filipino live sellers go live daily, vs a 31% regional average.** Filipino sellers spend up to **14 hours a week** live selling, vs up to 6 regionally. 52% say live selling is more profitable than marketplace listings. (Ninja Van white paper, 1,000+ sellers across SEA, [Jan 2023](https://blog.ninjavan.co/en-ph/live-selling-study-2023/), older)
- Across SEA, the top live-selling channels are Shopee (27.0%), Facebook (25.5%) and TikTok (22.5%). Sellers use two channels on average. (same Ninja Van study; regional, not PH-only)
- The Ken reported **73 million livestream shoppers** in the Philippines, with about 60% of online shoppers buying from livestream sellers, nearly double the regional average. ([The Ken, Aug 2023](https://the-ken.com/story/how-livestream-sellers-in-the-philippines-double-up-as-scammers-on-shopee-fb/), older)

**How it works today**

- Buyers comment "mine". The seller recognizes the first one and says "yours", then asks them to screenshot the item. Buyers are called "miners". ([Interaksyon](https://interaksyon.philstar.com/trends-spotlights/2022/08/09/224525/facebook-to-scrap-live-shopping-feature-in-october/amp/))
- Facebook's own Live Shopping tool was never offered in the Philippines. Sellers just use regular Facebook Live. ([GMA News, Aug 2022](https://gmanetwork.com/news/money/companies/841010/users-in-philippines-may-still-do-facebook-live-selling-meta/story))
- Orders are still captured by hand from comments and confirmed one by one in Messenger after the live. ([BigSeller, 2026](https://www.bigseller.pro/blog/articleDetails/4531/mastering-facebook-live-orders-2026-tips-for-filipino-sellers.htm))
- Payment is often COD: a 2022 Statista survey found **86% of Filipino online shoppers pay by COD**. ([Ninja Van, Jan 2024](https://blog.ninjavan.co/en-ph/how-to-reduce-cod-returns/))

**Seller pains, with evidence**

- **Time.** Up to 14 hours a week live, plus confirming each order by hand afterwards. Ninja Van lists "handling sales and post-sales arrangements" as one of three main live-selling challenges, without figures.
- **Joy reservers and bogus buyers.** Maya names them as a common scam on PH merchants: people who claim and back out, place fake orders, or reject delivered parcels. ([Maya, Apr 2024](https://www.maya.ph/business/stories/4-common-types-of-e-commerce-scams-that-affect-merchants-in-the-philippines)) No rate published.
- **COD returns.** Top return-to-sender reasons include wrong or incomplete details, no one home, and change of heart. ([Ninja Van, Jan 2024](https://blog.ninjavan.co/en-ph/how-to-reduce-cod-returns/)) No rate published.
- **Fake payment proofs.** A clothing live-seller lost nearly ₱230,000 to a bogus buyer. ([DZRH, Feb 2024](https://dzrh.com.ph/post/pnp-arrest-a-bogus-buyer-who-purchase-almost-p230000-worth-of-clothing-online)) An online tire seller lost over ₱1.4M in a month to fake bank-transfer receipts. ([GMA News, Oct 15 2026](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/metro/1006083/fake-proof-of-payments-used-in-p1-4-million-online-selling-scam/story/)) GCash warned about AI-made fake receipts and told users to check the Transactions tab. ([GCash, Jul 2025](https://mynt.com.ph/newsroom/gcash-warns-public-on-emerging-ai-generated-fake-receipts-scams-urges-users-to-always-check-transactions-tab))
- **Trust problems on the buyer side too.** DTI recorded **over 13,000 complaints about online transactions from January to August 2025**. ([Marketing-Interactive, Sept 2025](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/over-13-000-online-transaction-complaints-spur-mandatory-trustmark-registration)) The Ken found that over half of PH online shoppers had been targeted by fraudsters (2023).

**Rules that touch live sellers**

- **Internet Transactions Act (RA 11967)**, signed Dec 5, 2023: online merchants must disclose prices, deliver as agreed, follow data privacy law, and are primarily liable to consumers. It excludes consumer-to-consumer deals, and it doesn't clearly say how social media sellers are classified. ([Forvis Mazars](https://www.forvismazars.com/ph/en/insights/corporate-secretarial-alerts/internet-transactions-act-of-2023))
- **eCommerce Philippine Trustmark:** ₱1,130 a year. DTI deferred making it mandatory, kept it voluntary until end-2025, and said it would review in 2026. Barangay micro business enterprises (BMBEs) don't pay the fee. ([Inquirer, Oct 2025](https://business.inquirer.net/552900/e-commerce-trustmark-registration-deferred), [Philstar, Sept 2025](https://www.philstar.com/headlines/2025/09/22/2474537/online-sellers-slam-dtis-p1130-trustmark-fee/amp/)) Sellers pushed back hard on the fee. Whether it covers Facebook-only sellers is unclear.
- **BIR:** 1% withholding on half of remittances for sellers above ₱500,000 a year (RR 16-2023), covered in section 2.
- **Data privacy (RA 10173):** commenter names and addresses are personal data. Collect only what the order needs and delete after.

**Where the gap is**

- **After the live.** Capture is solved. Knowing which claims actually turned into verified payments is not. That's where the ₱230K and ₱1.4M losses happened.
- **Messy Taglish, not just keywords.** "Ate yung blue po", "pa-reserve", "cancel ko na": keyword tools catch "mine", but not intent changes and item descriptions.
- **Official data access exists.** Meta's Live Video API can stream a Page's live comments through an official endpoint ([Meta docs](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/live-video-api/interact-with-viewers)), so a compliant product doesn't need scraping.

**Slide-ready numbers**

- 475,000 video commerce sellers in the PH, up 90% in a year (e-Conomy SEA, 2025)
- 47% of Filipino live sellers go live daily, vs 31% across SEA (Ninja Van, 2023)
- Up to 14 hours a week spent live selling (Ninja Van, 2023)
- 13,000+ online transaction complaints to DTI in 8 months (DTI, 2025)
- ₱1.4M lost by one seller to fake payment proofs in a month (PNP via GMA, Oct 2026)

**What we couldn't find (go get it)**

- How many Filipino live sellers there are on Facebook specifically
- What share of "mine" claims never pay, the joy-miner rate
- How much time sellers spend confirming orders after a live
- Fix: Business 1 asks 5-10 live sellers 3 questions. How many claims per live? How many never pay? How many hours after the live? Those three answers are the strongest slide you can have.
